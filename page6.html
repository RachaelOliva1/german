German History

Germany’s history evolved from ancient Germanic tribes resisting Roman rule into the Holy Roman Empire, a fragmented realm of hundreds of states that lasted from 800 until 1806. Following the empire's collapse and a growing wave of nationalism, Prussia successfully unified these scattered territories into the modern German Empire in 1871 under Otto von Bismarck. This rising power's aggressive ambitions led to defeat in World War I, giving rise to the fragile Weimar Republic democracy, which eventually collapsed during the Great Depression. In 1933, Adolf Hitler seized power, establishing a totalitarian Nazi dictatorship that instigated World War II and perpetrated the Holocaust. Following its crushing defeat in 1945, the devastated nation was divided into democratic West Germany and communist East Germany throughout the Cold War. This division finally ended with the dramatic fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989 and formal reunification in 1990, paving the way for Germany to become a peaceful political and economic leader within the European Union today.
